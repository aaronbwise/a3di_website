Informing National Fortification Policy

Household grain consumption analysis from Namibia’s NHIES for the Iodine Global Network

Client
Iodine Global Network (IGN)
Sector
Food security & micronutrient fortification
Country
Namibia
Data
NHIES 2015–16 (12-month national survey)
Duration
Jul–Aug 2020
14 Regions
12 Months of data
4 Staple crops
5+ Stratifiers

Challenge

Namibia’s government was developing its Food and Nutrition Security Policy and Implementation Action Plan (2019–2024), which included mandatory fortification of staple foods as a strategy to address micronutrient deficiencies. But before deciding which foods to fortify, policymakers needed to understand which grains Namibian households actually consume, how consumption varies across regions and demographics, and whether households source their grain from own production or purchase it from markets (since only commercially processed grain can be fortified).

The Iodine Global Network commissioned A3DI to analyse microdata from the 2015–16 National Household Income and Expenditure Survey (NHIES), a 12-month nationally representative survey conducted by the Namibia Statistics Agency. The raw data included seven-day food diary entries linked to household demographics and income data, but had not been analysed for grain consumption patterns at the level of detail needed for fortification planning.

Approach

A3DI built a reproducible analysis pipeline in Python using Jupyter notebooks and a custom analytics module designed for weighted survey data. The module provided three core functions: weighted means, weighted medians, and a generalised disaggregation function that could stratify any outcome variable across multiple demographic and geographic dimensions.

NHIES cleaned CSV (12 months, all regions) ↓ Date reconstruction & filtering (Apr 2015 onward) ↓ Feature engineering (household IDs, survey weights, demographics) ↓ Three parallel analyses: 1. Crop production by region (maize, millet, sorghum, wheat) 2. Grain consumption patterns (own production vs. purchased) 3. Publication-ready visualisations by region and season

The analysis categorised food diary items into grain types (wheat products, mahangu/pearl millet, maize, sorghum) and tracked consumption sources to distinguish between own-production grain and market-purchased grain. This distinction is critical for fortification policy: only grain that passes through commercial milling can be fortified.

All calculations used the NHIES survey weights to produce nationally representative estimates. Results were disaggregated by region, urban/rural classification, education level, and household head demographics, then validated against Ministry of Agriculture administrative data and FAO Global Information and Early Warning System (GIEWS) figures.

Result

The analysis revealed significant regional variation in both grain consumption patterns and sourcing. Own-production consumption exceeded 45% for certain grain items in specific regions, indicating that a substantial share of household grain intake would not be reached by mandatory commercial fortification alone.

The disaggregated tables and visualisations showed which regions relied most heavily on which grains, and how consumption sources shifted across seasons and demographic groups. These findings gave policymakers the evidence they needed to target fortification strategies to the grain products and supply chains most likely to reach vulnerable populations.

The analysis was delivered as reproducible Jupyter notebooks with Excel workbook outputs (one sheet per crop variable per stratifier), enabling IGN and government stakeholders to explore the results directly and update the analysis as new survey data becomes available.

Key Takeaway

Fortification policy only works when it targets the foods people actually eat through the supply chains they actually use. Disaggregated consumption analysis from nationally representative surveys turns that question from a guess into an evidence-based decision.
