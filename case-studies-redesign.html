02 — The redesign strategy

Five principles behind the new layout

The fix is a layered page: the outcome lands in seconds for skimmers, and the technical depth you already wrote rewards anyone who digs in. Nothing here asks you to dumb down the work.

1

Lead with the outcome

Open every case study with the headline result as big, designed numbers — not the client/sector admin table. Earn the scroll in the first screen.

2

Layer for two readers

An "at-a-glance" band (problem / build / outcome) for the 20-second skim, with your full Challenge–Approach–Result prose beneath for the evaluator who reads everything.

3

Make the data visible

Turn your strongest figures into lightweight charts — triage splits, validation lift, consumption ranges. For a data practice, a chart is a portfolio piece.

4

Sell the capability left behind

Frame each result around the decision it enabled and the reusable asset it created — "a capability, not a one-off deliverable" — which is exactly how you already think.

5

Close with proof + a door

Add a trust signal (client quote or logo) and a single call to action at the end of every case study. An impressed reader should never hit a dead end.

6

One reusable system

Build it once as a template driven by content/data, so all three studies — and the next one — share a consistent, distinctive visual language.

01 Challenge

Manual review doesn't scale to a global database

The UNICEF/WHO/World Bank Joint Malnutrition Estimates maintain the global database of anthropometric surveys used to track child nutrition worldwide. Before a survey enters the published estimates, it must pass quality review — but with 1,000+ surveys and new rounds arriving constantly, hand review breaks down.

The existing process leaned on ad-hoc checks and expert judgement, making consistency hard to hold across reviewers and across years. Subtle issues — digit preference in measurements, age heaping, implausible z-score distributions, internally incoherent prevalence — are easy to miss without systematic screening.

The real requirement

An automated first pass that flags surveys with transparent evidence a human can act on — not a binary accept/reject machine that reviewers would never trust.

02 Approach

One survey in, 136 indicators, 5 independent verdicts

The pipeline computes 136 quality indicators per survey across 10 dimensions, then scores each survey through 5 independent statistical channels — each answering a different question about quality, so a flag always comes with a reason.

Why it earns a statistician's trust

Channels A–C judge how data was collected; E–F judge whether the resulting estimates are plausible — a survey can have clean measurements but implausible prevalence, or vice versa. The mixed-effects models nest by WHO × World Bank region (17 groups) with per-country random intercepts, so single-survey countries borrow strength from their region while long-history countries are judged against their own trajectory. Fitted parameters persist as Parquet — language-agnostic across Python, R, and Stata.

05 — How to build it

A reusable template, the way you build pipelines

Everything in the exemplar is plain HTML/CSS with zero JS and no chart library — the bars and diagrams are CSS. It'll drop into your current static setup and stay fast.

1

Separate content from layout

One template; each case study a small JSON/front-matter file (stats, glance, channels, chart data). Config-driven — the same pattern as your pipelines, so adding study #4 is data, not markup.

2

Promote a hero outcome block

Four headline stats + a one-line deck above the fold. The numbers already exist in your Result sections — they just need to move up and scale up.

3

Add the at-a-glance band

Problem / build / outcome in three short cells. This is the entire 20-second story; the prose below stays exactly as deep as it is now.

4

Chart the strongest figure

One visual per study — CSS bars here, or upgrade to inline SVG / a 5KB lib (uPlot, Chart.js) if you want tooltips. For a data practice, this is the highest-leverage change.

5

Add trust + a CTA component

A client quote or logo slot, and one reused call-to-action at the foot of every study. Even a paraphrased "enabled X decision" with attribution beats a silent ending.

6

Carry it to the homepage cards

Put each study's hero stat on its homepage card, so the impact shows before the click — not just a title and tags.
